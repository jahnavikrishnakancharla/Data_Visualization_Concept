## 📊 Visualizations Created

### 1. Number of Orders by Category

A Bar Chart was used to compare the number of orders across different product categories.

**Business Question:**

> Which category has the highest number of orders?

**Insight:**

* Clothing has the highest number of orders.
* Other has the lowest number of orders.
* Orders are concentrated more heavily in some categories.

---

### 2. Total Revenue by Category

Revenue was calculated by grouping the data by category and summing the order values.

**Business Question:**

> Which category generates the highest total revenue?

**Insight:**

* Clothing generated the highest total revenue.
* Clothing revenue was approximately **€17.66 million**.
* Other generated the lowest revenue at approximately **€3.26 million**.

---

### 3. Total Cost by Category

The total cost was calculated for each product category.

**Business Question:**

> How much total cost is associated with each category?

This helps compare the spending required for different categories.

---

### 4. Total Profit by Category

Profit was calculated using:

**Profit = Revenue − Cost**

**Business Question:**

> Which category generates the highest total profit?

**Insight:**

* Clothing generated the highest total profit.
* Clothing profit was approximately **€2.94 million**.
* Other generated the lowest total profit.

---

### 5. Profit Margin by Category

Profit margin was calculated using:

**Profit Margin = (Profit / Revenue) × 100**

Profit margin helps understand how efficiently revenue is converted into profit.

**Insight:**

* Outdoors had the highest profit margin at approximately **17.79%**.
* Clothing had a profit margin of approximately **16.66%**.
* Therefore, the category with the highest total profit is not necessarily the category with the highest profit margin.

---

## 📈 Key Findings

From the analysis:

1. **Clothing has the highest number of orders.**
2. **Clothing has the highest total revenue.**
3. **Clothing has the highest total profit.**
4. **Outdoors has the highest profit margin.**
5. High revenue does not necessarily mean high profit margin.
6. Different business metrics should be analysed together before making a decision.

## 📌 Important Formulas

### Revenue

Revenue is the total money generated from sales.

### Profit

```text
Profit = Revenue − Cost
```

### Profit Margin

```text
Profit Margin = (Profit / Revenue) × 100
```

## 💡 Business Insight

The dataset shows that **Clothing performs strongly in terms of order volume, revenue, and total profit**.

However, **Outdoors has a higher profit margin**, showing that total profit and profitability percentage are different measures.

Therefore, business decisions should consider multiple metrics rather than relying on a single visualization.
